## Purpose

This system turns Applied AI Services from a static storefront into a repeatable buyer-acquisition loop:

1. Find a named company with a visible operating leak.
2. Send a short, specific message offering the free Workflow Leak Snapshot.
3. Record the company and next action in Revenue OS CRM.
4. Convert useful snapshots into paid Operations Audits.
5. Convert proven audits into Automation Sprints or Governance Readiness.
6. Publish only approved, accurately labeled proof.

The service page is not expected to create demand by itself. Direct outreach, referral partners, proof-based content, and marketplace listings feed the page.

## First 30-day buyer

Primary profile:

- Founder-led business
- 5–50 employees or contractors
- One visible manual workflow
- A decision-maker is reachable
- The problem affects time, leads, revenue, customer response, reporting, or control
- The business can approve a $750–$1,500 diagnostic engagement

Initial geography and relationship advantage:

- Inland Empire
- San Bernardino County
- San Gabriel Valley
- Southern California
- Existing founder, construction, trucking, creator, startup, IEWBC, and business-service relationships

## Three campaign lanes

### Campaign A — Construction, trucking, and logistics

Target signals:

- Quote or dispatch requests handled by phone, email, or text
- Manual load, driver, site, or job coordination
- Repeated status calls
- Documents moving between office and field staff
- Invoice, ticket, or proof-of-delivery delays

Offer angle:

> Stop requests and job updates from disappearing between the phone, inbox, field, and office.

Free-scan example:

- Trigger: customer requests a quote or trucking capacity
- Leak: delayed assignment and inconsistent follow-up
- Human control: dispatcher or owner approves final commitment
- Evidence: request time, assigned owner, response, quote, exceptions, final status

### Campaign B — Professional and local service businesses

Target signals:

- Website form with no visible scheduling or follow-up path
- Owner personally answers every inquiry
- Intake repeated across email, spreadsheets, and CRM
- Manual proposal, onboarding, reporting, or reminder work
- Reviews mention slow response or communication

Offer angle:

> Make sure every qualified inquiry gets a fast response, a visible owner, and a next action.

Free-scan example:

- Trigger: new inquiry
- Leak: inbox delay and unclear ownership
- Human control: person approves price, scope, and final commitment
- Evidence: lead source, response time, assigned owner, next action, close/loss reason

### Campaign C — Creator, music, media, and startup businesses

Target signals:

- Content recreated for each channel
- Rights, approvals, assets, or versions scattered
- Royalty or payout reporting handled manually
- Founder is the approval bottleneck
- Product team has AI features without clear evidence or governance

Offer angle:

> Turn creation, approval, rights, and revenue events into one controlled path without losing ownership or voice.

Free-scan example:

- Trigger: new source asset or creator submission
- Leak: repeated editing, unclear approval, or missing usage record
- Human control: owner approves publication, rights, payout, or external commitment
- Evidence: source, permissions, versions, approval, usage event, payout or publication status

## Prospect selection standard

Do not add a prospect merely because the company exists.

A prospect should have at least one visible signal:

- Manual form, phone, email, or PDF workflow
- Hiring for administrative, operations, lead-generation, reporting, or coordination work
- Customer complaints about response or handoff
- Multiple disconnected tools
- Founder publicly discussing growth, backlog, missed follow-up, operational strain, or AI adoption
- A warm introduction or existing relationship

Record the actual signal. Never invent a pain point.

## Daily operating cadence

Weekdays:

1. Research 5 new named companies.
2. Move 5 qualified records from `new` to `targeted`.
3. Send 10 personalized first messages or follow-ups.
4. Generate up to 2 useful snapshots for prospects who provide enough detail.
5. Update every touched lead before stopping.
6. Publish or prepare one proof-based content asset three days per week.

Do not send mass generic messages. Ten specific contacts are more valuable than one hundred AI-agency pitches.

## Outreach sequence

### First message

> Hey [Name] — I looked at [Company] and noticed [specific visible workflow or signal]. That kind of handoff can make leads or requests depend too much on the inbox and memory. I built a free Workflow Leak Snapshot that shows the likely leak, what could be automated, what should remain human-controlled, and what evidence the finished workflow should leave. I can send the link, or map the first snapshot with you. Worth it?

### Follow-up 1 — 3 business days later

> Circling back because the goal is not to sell you a giant AI build. It is to identify one workflow that is losing time, leads, or control and show the smallest credible fix. Here is the free scan: [link].

### Follow-up 2 — 5 business days later

> Last note from me. The best starting point for [Company] may be [one-sentence hypothesis]. If that is not a priority right now, no problem—I will close the loop.

### Reply when interested

> Perfect. Use the scan for one workflow only and avoid confidential customer records or credentials. Once it generates the snapshot, send it to me and I will review whether the next step should be a paid audit, a scoped sprint, or no build at all.

### Audit close

> Based on the workflow you described, the honest next step is the AI Operations Audit at [price]. The audit will map the current path, quantify the leak, define approvals and exceptions, and leave a 30-day implementation plan. It does not commit you to a build.

## Content engine

Publish three proof-based posts each week.

### Monday — expose the leak

Structure:

1. Name a common broken workflow.
2. Explain where the handoff fails.
3. State what the business should measure.
4. Invite the free scan.

### Wednesday — redesign the path

Structure:

1. Show the trigger.
2. Show the automated support.
3. Show the human approval.
4. Show the evidence left afterward.

### Friday — publish proof or limitation

Structure:

1. Show a route, receipt, test, diagram, or operating artifact.
2. Label it internal or client-approved external proof.
3. State the known limitation.
4. State the next hardening step.

Never post generic claims such as “AI changes everything.” Show an operating path.

## Objection log

Record objections in CRM notes using one of these categories:

- No urgency
- No budget
- Problem unclear
- Wants guaranteed outcome
- Wants unsupported cybersecurity service
- Wants certification or legal conclusion
- No decision-maker access
- Data/access dependency unresolved
- Prefers internal team
- Timing later
- Trust/proof concern
- Price concern
- No response

Every five repeated objections should trigger a review of the offer, proof page, FAQ, or qualification language.

## Proof conversion rule

After every delivery, record:

- What the client said was broken
- What was included
- What changed
- What was measured
- What remained human-controlled
- What failed during testing
- What remains limited
- Whether the client approved a quote, logo, anonymized summary, or no publication

Only approved proof may move to `/services/results`.
