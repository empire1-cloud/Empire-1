# Empire-1 Programming & Development Radar

## Purpose

The Programming & Development Radar converts recurring developer-content structures into original, proof-backed Empire-1 hooks and series plans.

It is available at `/admin/gtm` under **Dev Radar**.

This is a pattern library, not a scraper and not a copying system. Operators supply the Empire product, workflow, available proof, and desired outcome. The radar produces original hooks based on reusable editorial structures.

## Headline modes

### Future Shock

Formula:

> [Old role or workflow] is changing. Here is what replaces it.

Use when a dated technology or behavior change has already been observed.

### Real Bottleneck

Formula:

> AI can do [task]. That was never the bottleneck.

Use when the obvious technical task is solved but coordination, trust, ownership, distribution, or proof remains unsolved.

### Reality Check

Formula:

> [Time period] in: what survived contact with reality.

Use after repeated runs, real projects, retention evidence, defect reviews, or operator notes.

### Teardown

Formula:

> Why [system] is broken: we optimized the wrong thing.

Use only when the mechanism and failure mode can be explained.

### Missing Layer

Formula:

> [Framework] gets these parts right. It still misses the layer that decides success.

Separate sourced framework claims from Empire-1 interpretation.

### Actual Workflow

Formula:

> How I actually use [system] to ship [outcome].

Show inputs, steps, approvals, artifacts, and final proof.

## Five-day challenge funnel

The radar includes a reusable five-day content sequence:

1. **Thinking Before Tools** — define the problem and named framework.
2. **The System** — show workflow, handoffs, guardrails, and proof standard.
3. **The Build** — publish a demo, teardown, or before-and-after.
4. **The Agent or Engine** — explain model, engine, and operator roles.
5. **The Outcome** — connect proof to revenue, ownership, community impact, or customer value.

Every day should produce an owned asset such as a framework card, checklist, receipt, process map, short video, or pilot invitation.

## Provider independence

The source inspiration may discuss named commercial AI providers. Empire-1 remains provider-neutral and must not create a Google or Gemini dependency.

Allowed implementation direction:

- Empire-1-owned orchestration
- Approved non-Google providers
- Local or open models where appropriate
- Explicit role maps that can swap providers without changing the product identity

## Reverse-engineering rule

Reverse engineering means studying structure, workflow, and decision patterns. It does not mean copying protected content, branding, prompts, or finished assets.

The radar should extract:

- headline structure
- sequence design
- proof placement
- funnel progression
- reusable asset strategy

It should generate new Empire-1 content from the founder's own facts and evidence.

## Integrity rules

- Never reuse another creator's follower, revenue, conversion, or performance claims as Empire-1 proof.
- Do not describe a trend as current without a dated source or observation.
- Label founder interpretation separately from verified evidence.
- Preserve each Empire product universe and its own proof standard.
- Keep provider choices replaceable and independent of Google/Gemini APIs.
- Do not publish a teardown without explaining the mechanism and alternative.
